## Rationale

**No blocker is supported by the snapshot text.** The rollout order is sound:

- Clients get a dual-root bundle on day 1 (line 4).
- Bundles are re-read in place every 15 minutes without closing open connections (line 2).
- Fleet-wide coverage is confirmed on day 3 (line 5).
- The server switches to the 'Internal Root 2026' certificate, with the full chain, on day 7 (line 6).
- 'Internal Root 2023' is removed from bundles on day 30 (line 8). By then the server has not presented a 2023-chained certificate for 23 days, so the old certificate still being valid until day 60 (line 3) causes no gap.
- Destroying the old root's private key on day 30 has no effect on clients' ability to verify the new chain.

**Two items still deserve attention:**

1. **Fixed date versus gate (RISK, VERIFIED).** Line 5 says the switch only gets a date after the day-3 inventory shows every client covering both roots. Lines 3 and 6 already fix the switch at day 7. The text is inconsistent: if the inventory finds a straggler, the plan has no stated path to delay the switch. A client without the 2026 root would then lose connectivity, which breaks the requirement in line 1.
2. **Shared subject name (RISK, UNVERIFIED).** Line 7 says the two roots share a subject name and differ only by key identifier. Some TLS stacks choose an issuer by subject name and may pick the wrong root. The only evidence of correct behaviour is from staging. That does not show that every production client implementation picks the right root. This is missing evidence, not a proven defect.
