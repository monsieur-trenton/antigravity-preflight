**Rationale**

The snapshot's own text does not support a blocker. The rollback job was timed three times on a production-size copy (line 6) at 5, 6 and 6 minutes. That fits inside the 15-minute limit (lines 2–3) with a wide margin. Line 5 explains why dropping the column does not hurt 7.2. Line 7 gives a reason for skipping the canary stage. Nothing in the plan contradicts itself in a way that would stop the release.

Some gaps should still be closed:

- **When the outage clock starts (line 2).** The clock "runs until the prior version is serving traffic again", but the plan never says when it starts. The 15-minute limit is measured from the moment the release is declared failed. If the contract clock starts when the outage begins, the time spent detecting and declaring the failure counts against the 15 minutes. The plan doesn't account for that time. This is unverified, not a proven defect.
- **Test conditions for the rollback timing (line 6).** The timing was done on a database copy. The plan doesn't say whether the copy was under production-like traffic. Dropping a column usually needs an exclusive table lock. Under live load, waiting for that lock could make the rollback much slower than the measured 5–6 minutes.
- **Lost discount codes (line 5).** Dropping the column permanently deletes every discount_code that 7.3 writes before the rollback. Line 5 only argues that 7.2 does not need this data. It does not say whether the business needs it, or whether it should be backed up before the drop. This is a note rather than a defect.
