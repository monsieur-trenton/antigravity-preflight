**Rationale**

The arithmetic is internally consistent:
- 4 workers × 1.25 req/s = 5 req/s.
- 240,000 ÷ 5 req/s = 48,000 s, about 13.3 h.
- The peak is 5 + 3 = 8 req/s.
- The daily maximum is 240,000 + 50,000 = 290,000 requests.

The blocker comes from a conflict between two lines. Line 2 makes staying within the provider's rate limits "at all times" a hard requirement, because a breach would also cut off signup lookups. Line 6 says nobody knows what those limits are, and the plan gives no figure for them. So the plan's computed peaks (8 req/s, 290,000/day) cannot be compared with anything. Its central safety requirement cannot be shown to hold. The text itself supports this, so it is a blocker. It is not an inference from missing evidence about some other matter.

Two lesser issues:
- **Line 4 (risk, unverified):** The signup figures are historical maxima from the last 6 months, not guaranteed caps. Real traffic during the run could exceed 3 req/s or 50,000/day.
- **Line 7 (note):** Not resending failed lookups keeps the request count bounded. However, failed addresses stay ungeocoded, and the plan does not say how they are tracked or completed later.
