**Rationale**

No blocker is supported by the snapshot's own text. The plan holds together on the key constraint:

- **Rollback timing fits the policy.** Line 2 requires full rollback within 15 minutes of being declared failed. Line 4 says the rollback job starts automatically at that moment. Line 6 says three timed runs of the complete job took 5 to 6 minutes until 7.2 served traffic. That leaves about 9 minutes of margin, and the timings already include the down-migration on a production-sized copy.
- **The down-migration is safe for 7.2.** Line 5 says 7.2 never reads or writes `discount_code`, so dropping the column cannot break 7.2.
- **Skipping the canary is justified.** Line 7 says 7.2 has served all production traffic for the past 30 days.

Some risks remain, but none is a proven defect:

1. **Test conditions may not match production (line 6).** The timings come from a copy, not from live production. Live traffic, lock contention and replication lag could slow the column drop on a busy 400 GB table. The snapshot gives no evidence about behaviour under load, and line 3 makes overrunning 15 minutes costly.
2. **Rolling back loses data written by 7.3 (line 5).** Line 5 only shows that 7.2 does not need the column. Any discount codes recorded by 7.3 before the rollback would be permanently discarded. The snapshot does not say whether that data matters to the business or the merchant.
3. **No fallback if the job fails (line 4).** Rollback depends on one automatic pipeline job. The plan names no manual fallback if the job fails partway, for example after the down-migration but before 7.2 is redeployed.
