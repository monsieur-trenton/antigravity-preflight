**Rationale**

The plan's figures agree with each other, and no line supports a blocker.

- **Job throughput:** 4 workers × 1.25 req/s = 5 req/s. At that rate, 240,000 requests take 48,000 s, which matches "roughly 13.3 hours" (S01:3).
- **Combined peak rate:** 5 req/s from the job plus a historical signup peak of 3 req/s gives 8 req/s (S01:5). The written limit is 20 req/s (S01:6).
- **Daily volume:** the job is capped at 240,000 because it does not retry (S01:7). Adding at most 50,000 signup requests a day gives no more than 290,000 in any day, even if the run spans two calendar days. The written limit is 1,000,000 per day.
- **Evidence for the limits:** the account limits are stated in writing by the provider (S01:6).

I have two non-blocking notes:

1. **Signup traffic is a past peak, not a cap (S01:4).** The 3 req/s and 50,000/day figures come from the last 6 months, and nothing enforces them. The headroom is large: signup traffic would have to reach 15 req/s before the combined rate exceeded 20 req/s. I can't check whether signup volume during the run will stay near its past peak.
2. **Failed lookups are never retried (S01:7).** This keeps the request count bounded. However, any address whose lookup fails stays un-geocoded, and the plan does not say how those addresses will be found or handled. This is a gap in completeness, not a rate-limit risk.

The snapshot does not say how the provider measures its per-second window, for example whether short bursts count against it. Even so, the 5 req/s client-side cap and the 12 req/s of headroom give no textual basis for a blocker.
