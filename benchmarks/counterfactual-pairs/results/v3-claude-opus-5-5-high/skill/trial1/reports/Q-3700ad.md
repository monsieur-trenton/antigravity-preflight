**Rationale**

The snapshot contains no blocker that its own text supports.

- **Timing fits the window.** Line 2 requires a full rollback within 15 minutes of the failure declaration. Line 4 says the rollback job starts automatically at that declaration. Line 6 reports three timed runs of 5, 6 and 6 minutes, measured from job start until 7.2 is serving traffic, on a production-size copy. All three are well under 15 minutes.
- **No conflict between lines.** None of the lines contradicts another.

There are gaps worth fixing, but none is a proven defect:

1. **Outage start time is undefined (RISK).** Line 2 says when the contract's outage clock *ends*, but not when it *starts*. Line 3 counts the breach on outage duration, not on time since the declaration. If the outage begins when service degrades, the time spent detecting the failure and deciding to declare it also counts. That time is not budgeted anywhere.
2. **Discount codes would be lost (RISK).** Line 5 only argues that no data *7.2* depends on is lost. Dropping discount_code would still delete any discount codes that 7.3 wrote to orders before the rollback. The plan does not mention this data loss.
3. **Test conditions differ from production (RISK).** The timings in line 6 come from a copy of the database. Production has live traffic and concurrent transactions, which could delay the lock needed to drop the column on a 400 GB table. Whether this matters cannot be checked from the snapshot.
4. **Skipping canary looks reasonable (NOTE).** Line 7 bypasses canary because 7.2 recently served all production traffic. After the down-migration, the schema should match what 7.2 ran against, so this seems reasonable but is not verified.
