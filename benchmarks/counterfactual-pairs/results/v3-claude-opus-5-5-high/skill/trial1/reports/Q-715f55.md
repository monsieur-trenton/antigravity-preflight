**Rationale**

The snapshot contains no blocker. The only candidate is the 5,000,000-byte body limit (line 1), given that the notes field has no length limit (line 3). Lines 2 and 5 resolve it:

- **The data is fixed and fully scanned.** The source is a closed snapshot that no longer changes. A scan of every record found a maximum serialized size of 4,100 bytes.
- **The arithmetic holds.** 500 × 4,100 = 2,050,000 bytes. Adding the envelope and separators (under 1,000 bytes, line 4) gives a ceiling under 2,051,000 bytes. That is well under the 5,000,000-byte limit.
- **The timeout is adequate.** 120 seconds covers the partner's documented 60-second worst case (line 8).

These are recommended changes rather than blockers:

1. **Risk (lines 6–7): no recovery from a failed send.** The job sends each batch once and never resends, so any lost batch makes reconciliation fail. That could be a transient error, a timeout or a lost response. The month then stays open until the next cycle. The no-retry rule is justified by the duplicate-filing concern. However, the plan gives no way to check whether the partner actually received a batch. It also gives no idempotent or controlled recovery path.
2. **Note (line 5): the size bound depends on the scan.** The scan covers one closed snapshot, and the notes field is unbounded. The plan does not say the scan is repeated for each month-end run, or that the job checks batch size before sending. If later months are not rescanned, the 2,050,000-byte bound does not carry over.
