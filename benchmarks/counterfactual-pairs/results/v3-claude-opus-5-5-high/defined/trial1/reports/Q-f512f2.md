# Review: Address Backfill Geocoding Plan

## The arithmetic is correct

| Claim | Check | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Job throughput | 4 workers × 1.25 req/s | 5 req/s ✓ |
| Job duration | 240,000 ÷ 5 = 48,000 s | ≈ 13.3 h ✓ |
| Combined peak rate | 5 (job) + 3 (signup peak) | 8 req/s ✓ |
| Max requests in one day | 240,000 (the whole job, at most) + 50,000 (signup peak) | 290,000 ✓ |
| Job total bounded | No retries, so failures add nothing | ≤ 240,000 ✓ |

## Blocking issue: the plan cannot show it meets its own central requirement

The plan sets a hard rule: **stay within the provider's rate limits at all times.** It then says nobody has confirmed what those limits are, and it gives no figure for them.

The plan measures its own load carefully: 8 req/s at peak and 290,000 requests per day at most. But it compares those numbers against nothing. Without the actual limits, there is no basis for claiming the run stays within them. Common limits that 8 req/s or 290k/day could easily breach include:

- per-second limits of 5 req/s or lower,
- per-minute limits,
- daily quotas of 100k or 250k, and
- monthly quotas, which the plan does not consider at all.

The consequence is severe and spreads beyond the job. A suspension would also stop live address lookups during signup. So the stated rule is effectively a precondition for running, and the plan cannot show that it holds.

## Secondary issues

1. **The signup figures are past peaks, not caps.** "Topped out at 3 req/s and 50,000/day over 6 months" does not guarantee the same during the run. A marketing push or a traffic spike could exceed them. Nothing in the plan limits signup traffic or pauses the backfill when signup load rises.
2. **There is no shared or global limiter.** Each worker limits itself independently, and nothing coordinates the job with signup traffic. If someone starts a fifth worker, restarts a worker mid-run, or runs two job instances by accident, the 5 req/s ceiling breaks without anyone noticing.
3. **Limiter burst behaviour is not specified.** A token-bucket limiter at 1.25 req/s with a burst size above 1 can briefly exceed the average. If the provider enforces short windows, such as per-second limits, those bursts matter.
4. **There is no reaction to provider signals.** The plan does not say what happens when the provider returns 429 or quota-warning responses. The job should stop or back off at once, not keep sending.
5. **There is no headroom.** Even after the limits are known, the plan should target a margin below them, for example 70–80%, rather than run close to the ceiling.

## Required before proceeding

- Get the account's actual limits from the provider, in writing or from the account dashboard: per-second, per-minute, daily, and monthly. Put the figures in the plan and show that 8 req/s and 290k/day fit within them with margin.
- If they don't fit, reduce the worker count or per-worker rate, or spread the run over several days.
- Add a circuit breaker that halts the job on 429 or quota-warning responses. Ideally also throttle the backfill when signup traffic rises, so signup keeps priority.

Because the plan's own non-negotiable rule cannot be shown to hold as written, it should not run yet.
