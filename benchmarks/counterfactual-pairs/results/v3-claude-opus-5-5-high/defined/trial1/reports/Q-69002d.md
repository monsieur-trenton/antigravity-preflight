# Review: Address Backfill Geocoding Plan

The plan's stated rule is that the account must never exceed the provider's rate limits. I checked each figure against the confirmed limits of 20 req/s and 1,000,000 req/day.

**Per-second rate**
- The job sends at most 4 workers × 1.25 req/s = 5 req/s.
- Signup has peaked at 3 req/s over 6 months.
- Combined peak is 8 req/s, which is 40% of the 20 req/s limit. Signup traffic could more than quadruple before the combined rate reached the limit.

**Run duration**
- 240,000 requests ÷ 5 req/s = 48,000 s ≈ 13.3 hours, as the plan says.

**Daily volume**
- A 13.3-hour run may cross midnight. Even so, the job sends at most 240,000 requests in any calendar day, because it never sends more than that in total.
- Signup has peaked at 50,000 per day, so the worst day is 290,000 requests, which is 29% of the 1,000,000 limit.

**Total request bound**
- With no retries, the job can't send more than 240,000 requests, so retry storms can't push it over the limits.

**Bursts**
- Client-side limiters can allow short bursts. With 12 req/s of headroom per second, a brief burst from all four workers would still stay well under 20 req/s.

All the arithmetic is correct, and every limit has a wide margin. Nothing in the plan breaks its own rule or needs to change for the run to succeed safely.

**Optional improvements (not required):**
- Write failed lookups to a file or table so they can be handled in a later, separately limited run.
- If the job crashes and is restarted, make sure it skips addresses that were already geocoded. Otherwise the total could exceed 240,000. Even so, the daily margin is large enough to absorb a full rerun (240,000 + 240,000 + 50,000 = 530,000).
- Have the job stop or slow down on HTTP 429 responses, as a final safeguard in case the provider measures rates differently than expected.
