# Review: Nightly Batch Plan

## The core problem: the plan cannot meet its deadline

The stages run one after another, so the total runtime is the sum of the stage times:

| Scenario | Import | Transform | Export | **Total** | vs. 45 min deadline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Typical | 22 | 18 | 12 | **52 min** | 7 min over |
| Worst observed | 25 | 21 | 14 | **60 min** | 15 min over |

Even a typical run overruns by about 15%. The fastest possible run would need every stage to beat its average by 7 minutes combined, which nothing in the data suggests. As written, **nearly every run will be killed and that day's data lost.** This is not an edge-case risk; it is the expected outcome.

## Secondary issues

1. **Failure alerting is unclear.** The summary email goes out "when the batch ends." If the run is killed at 45 minutes, it is not clear whether the email fires. Under this plan the kill is the normal path, so on-call might be told nothing on most nights. Alerting needs to be triggered externally, for example by a watchdog or the scheduler's kill hook, not by the batch itself.
2. **No headroom even after a fix.** Shaving a few minutes off to reach 44–45 is not enough. Using the worst-case figures, the plan needs a real margin of at least 10–15%.
3. **The measurements don't match the situation.** The plan cites 30 measured runs, yet all of those would have overrun a 45-minute limit. Either the deadline is new, the stages were measured separately or in a different environment, or runs are already failing. That should be explained before anything else is decided.
4. **No recovery path.** "Data is lost" on overrun suggests there are no checkpoints or retries. Even after the timing is fixed, losing a full day of data on a single slow night is a fragile design.

## Changes needed before this can go ahead

- **Bring the worst-case total under about 40 minutes.** Options include:
  - Pipelining or overlapping stages, for example streaming import output into transform in chunks, if the data allows it.
  - Parallelizing within stages: partitioning import or export.
  - Optimizing the import stage, which takes the most time.
  - Negotiating a longer window or an earlier start time.
- **Add checkpointing** so a killed run can resume, or at least keep the completed stages instead of losing the day.
- **Send alerts independently of batch completion**, covering both success and failure or kill, and include stage timings so drift shows up early.
- **Set a per-stage time budget** so on-call is warned before the hard kill, not after it.

## Verdict

The plan fails its main requirement on typical runs, not just worst-case ones. This needs a redesign, not small adjustments, so it should not proceed as written.
