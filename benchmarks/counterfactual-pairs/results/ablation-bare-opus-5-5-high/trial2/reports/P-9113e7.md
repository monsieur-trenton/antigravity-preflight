# Review: Nightly Batch Plan

## The core problem: the plan does not fit the deadline

The stages run in sequence, so their durations add up.

| Scenario | Import | Transform | Export | **Total** | vs. 45 min |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Typical | 22 | 18 | 12 | **52 min** | 7 min over |
| Worst observed | 25 | 21 | 14 | **60 min** | 15 min over |

Even a typical night runs about 7 minutes past the deadline. The kill would hit partway through export on essentially every run. Overrunning means the day's data is lost, so as written this plan loses data nightly. This is not an edge case. It is the expected outcome.

To fit worst-case durations in 45 minutes, you would need to cut **at least 15 minutes**, plus some safety margin. A margin of about 10–20% (5–9 min) is reasonable, since 30 runs is a modest sample and the maximum can grow.

## Secondary issues

1. **Failure notification is unclear.** The summary email goes out "when the batch ends." If the run is killed at 45 minutes, does that path still fire? If the email comes from the batch process, a hard kill probably suppresses it, and on-call never learns data was lost. Send the alert from an external watchdog or scheduler, and alert explicitly on kill or timeout.
2. **No partial-progress protection.** Because of the "killed and data lost" rule, 40+ minutes of completed import and transform work is thrown away. Persisting each stage's output, with checkpoints so a rerun can resume, would make overruns recoverable.
3. **No recovery path.** The plan does not say whether a lost day can be re-run later, such as in a daytime window or the next night. That needs to be defined.

## Options to make it viable (one or more needed)

- **Change the constraint:** extend the window to at least 70 minutes if the 45-minute limit isn't truly fixed.
- **Overlap or parallelize:** pipeline the stages (for example, stream transformed batches into export while transform continues), or partition the data and run shards in parallel. Revisit the "never overlap" rule if it isn't a hard requirement.
- **Speed up stages:** profile import, the largest at 22–25 minutes, first. Consider incremental or delta processing instead of full reloads.
- **Start earlier or split the job:** move import to an earlier window, or run part of it intraday.
- **Make overruns survivable:** add checkpointing and resume so a kill doesn't discard completed work.

Before re-approving, re-measure the revised plan's worst case and confirm it lands comfortably under 45 minutes.
