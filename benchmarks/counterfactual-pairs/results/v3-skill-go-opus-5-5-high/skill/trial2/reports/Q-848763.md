**Rationale**

The snapshot's own text shows a lock-order inversion that can deadlock:

- **Line 2:** checkout takes `stock_mu` and then `reserve_mu` while still holding `stock_mu`.
- **Line 3:** restock takes `reserve_mu` and then `stock_mu` while still holding `reserve_mu`.
- **Line 4:** both paths often run at the same time on separate threads.
- **Line 1:** neither acquisition has a timeout.

So checkout can hold `stock_mu` and wait for `reserve_mu` while restock holds `reserve_mu` and waits for `stock_mu`. Neither can proceed, and nothing in the text breaks the cycle. Line 6 rules out any other acquisition that could change this. Line 7 says a deadlock is never acceptable. This is a blocker, and the text alone verifies it.

The nightly audit job (line 5) holds only `stock_mu`, for at most 2 seconds, and takes no other lock. It can briefly delay the other paths but cannot join a deadlock cycle, so it is not a finding.

A likely fix is to make restock acquire `stock_mu` before `reserve_mu`, matching checkout's order. Alternatives are a single combined lock or a try-lock with back-off.
