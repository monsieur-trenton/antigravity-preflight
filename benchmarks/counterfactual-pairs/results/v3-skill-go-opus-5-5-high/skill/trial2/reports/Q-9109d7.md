**Rationale**

The snapshot contains no blocker supported by its own text.

- **Retention covers the recovery window.** Line 1 requires recovery up to 14 days back. Line 4 correctly says this can need a base backup up to 21 days old plus all WAL after it. Line 5 keeps both backups and WAL for 28 days, which exceeds 21. Daily deletion timing does not cut into that margin.
- **Permissions do not conflict.** Line 6 gives the lifecycle job delete permission. Line 7 says the database hosts cannot overwrite or delete existing objects. These apply to different principals, so the lines are consistent.
- **The rehearsal tests the oldest part of the window.** Line 8 restores to a point 13–14 days back, which exercises the deepest base-backup and WAL dependency.

There are a few unverified risks:

1. **The 5-minute target may not hold during quiet periods.** Line 2 says each WAL segment is archived within 60 seconds of being written. That timing only helps the 5-minute target in line 1 if segments are switched regularly. In a quiet period, a partly filled segment may not be archived for a long time unless something like a forced segment switch on a timer is configured. The snapshot does not say whether it is.
2. **The lifecycle job is a deletion path into an otherwise protected bucket.** Line 6 limits its deletes only through a matching rule, not through a permission boundary. If that rule is misconfigured or the job is compromised, it could delete backups that are still needed. This is unverified.
3. **The rehearsal does not cover the recent end of the window.** Line 8 never tests recovery to about 5 minutes ago. This is a gap in evidence, not a proven defect.
