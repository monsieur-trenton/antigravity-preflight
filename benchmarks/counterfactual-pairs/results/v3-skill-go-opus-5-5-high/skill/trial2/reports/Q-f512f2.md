**Rationale**

The snapshot's arithmetic is internally consistent:
- **Duration:** 4 workers × 1.25 req/s = 5 req/s. 240,000 ÷ 5 = 48,000 s, which is about 13.3 h (line 3).
- **Peak rate:** 5 req/s from the job plus a historical signup peak of 3 req/s gives 8 req/s (line 5).
- **Daily volume:** The job cannot exceed 240,000 requests in total, because line 7 rules out resends. Adding 50,000 signup requests gives at most 290,000 in any day (line 5).

**Main finding (risk, not blocker)**

Line 2 sets a hard requirement: the account must stay within its rate limits at all times. Line 6 states that the applicable limits are unknown and that the plan contains no figure for them. The plan therefore cannot show that it meets its own requirement. However, nothing in the snapshot shows that 8 req/s or 290,000 requests per day actually exceeds any limit. Under the review contract, missing evidence is not a proven defect. Whether a breach would occur cannot be checked from the snapshot, so this is a significant risk. It should be resolved before the run, given the stated consequence: suspension would also cut off live signup lookups.

**Secondary notes**
- **Line 4:** The signup peaks are historical maxima from the last 6 months. They do not guarantee future signup traffic.
- **Line 7:** Because failed lookups are not resent, some of the 240,000 addresses may be left without geocoding. The plan does not say how those gaps would be handled.

No line establishes a blocker.
