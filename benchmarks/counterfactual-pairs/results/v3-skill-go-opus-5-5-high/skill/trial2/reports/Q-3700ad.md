**Rationale**

The snapshot's own text does not support a blocker. The rollback job was measured at 5, 6 and 6 minutes on a production-size copy (line 6). That is well inside the 15-minute limit in lines 2–3. Line 5 shows the down-migration loses nothing that 7.2 depends on. Line 7 gives a reasonable basis for skipping the canary stage. Some gaps remain, so changes are recommended:

1. **When the outage clock starts (RISK, UNVERIFIED, line 2).** The 5–6 minutes are measured from the job's start (line 6). The job starts only when the release is declared failed (line 4). Line 2 says the contract clock runs until 7.2 serves traffic again, but it never says when the clock starts. If it starts when the outage begins, the time spent detecting and declaring the failure also counts. The real margin could then be much smaller than 9–10 minutes. The plan should state the clock's start point and budget for detection and declaration time.

2. **Rehearsal conditions (RISK, CANNOT_CHECK, line 6).** The timings come from a copy without live traffic. In production, dropping a column on a 400 GB table may have to wait for a table lock while other transactions are running. Rollback time could then exceed the rehearsal times. The snapshot gives no evidence about behaviour under production load or about lock or statement timeouts.

3. **Discount codes are deleted on rollback (NOTE, VERIFIED, line 5).** Line 5 rules out loss of data that 7.2 needs. But dropping the column deletes any discount codes that orders received while 7.3 was live. The plan does not say whether that business data matters or should be kept before the drop.
