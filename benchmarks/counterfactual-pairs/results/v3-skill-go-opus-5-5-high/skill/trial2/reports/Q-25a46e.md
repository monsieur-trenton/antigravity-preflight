**Rationale**

The plan's own numbers break its requirement. Line 3 fixes the fleet at exactly 12 independent, uncoordinated workers. Line 4 lets each worker send up to 5 requests in any one-second window. Together they allow 12 × 5 = 60 requests per second. Line 2 sets a combined ceiling of 50 requests per second and suspends the key if any one-second window goes over it.

With 3.2 million rows to process (line 1), the workers can be expected to run near their caps. The combined rate can therefore exceed 50 per second by up to 10 requests per second. That breaks the requirement in line 7 and risks a 24-hour suspension that would also stop live checkout lookups. The check uses only stated figures, so it is VERIFIED.

The other lines do not hide or reduce this problem:
- Line 5 says retries use the same limiter allowance, so they cannot add load beyond the per-worker cap. They also cannot lower it.
- Line 6 rules out other users of the key, so the 60 per second comes entirely from the backfill.

To fix this, the per-worker cap must be at most 4 (12 × 4 = 48), or rate limiting must be coordinated across all workers.
