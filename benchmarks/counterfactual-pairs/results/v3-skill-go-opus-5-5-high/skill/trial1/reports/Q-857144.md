The snapshot does not support a blocker. Line 2 sets a 15-minute rollback limit. Line 4 says rollback starts automatically when the release is declared failed. There are still some gaps worth fixing:

1. **When the contract's outage clock starts is not stated (line 2).** Line 2 measures the 15 minutes from when the release is "declared failed". It only says the contract's outage clock runs *until* 7.2 serves traffic again, not when it starts. Line 3 makes any outage over 15 minutes a breach. If the contract clock starts when the outage begins rather than when it is declared, the time taken to detect and declare the failure uses up the headroom. The snapshot gives no figure for that time, so this is unverified and not a proven defect.
2. **The timing comes from a copy, not live production (line 6).** The runs used a production-sized copy. The snapshot does not say whether they ran under production-like traffic. Dropping a column on a 400 GB table that is serving live traffic may wait on locks or contend with that traffic in ways an idle copy does not show. The headroom makes this less likely to matter, but it is still unverified.
3. **Rollback deletes data written by 7.3 (line 5).** Line 5 shows only that 7.2 does not need the column. Any discount codes recorded while 7.3 was live are permanently lost on rollback. The snapshot does not say whether that is acceptable for re-release, billing or audit purposes.
