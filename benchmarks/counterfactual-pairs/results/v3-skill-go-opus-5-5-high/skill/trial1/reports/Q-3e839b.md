**Rationale**

No blocker is supported by the snapshot. The sequence holds together on its own terms:

- **Trust before switch.** Every client gets the dual-root bundle on day 1 (line 4). The bundle reloads in place every 15 minutes (line 2). The day-3 inventory must show every client holding both roots before the switch is scheduled (line 5). So every client trusts 'Internal Root 2026' before the server presents it on day 7 (line 6).
- **Old certificate stays valid long enough.** The 'Internal Root 2023' certificate is served only until day 7 and is valid until day 60 (line 3), so it does not expire mid-rollout.
- **Removing the old root comes after the switch.** 'Internal Root 2023' is dropped on day 30 (line 8), 23 days after the server stopped presenting it. Bundle reloads do not close open connections (line 2), so dropping the root does not break live sessions.

There are two residual points, neither of them a blocker:

1. **Same subject name (line 7).** The two roots share a subject name and differ only by key identifier. Some chain builders choose an issuer by name, so this case can fail. The plan cites staging success, but nothing shows that the staging clients cover every production client implementation. This remains unverified rather than a proven defect.
2. **No rollback after day 30 (line 8).** Destroying the 'Internal Root 2023' private key on day 30 removes any path back to the old root. That is an intentional, irreversible step and worth acknowledging, but nothing in the plan conflicts with it.
