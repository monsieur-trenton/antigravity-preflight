**Rationale**

The plan contains one blocker, and its own lines support it.

- **The rule:** Line 2 says no capture may be applied twice.
- **How retries break it:** Line 3 retries the same capture up to 5 times. Line 4 says a retry can arrive after the server has already applied the capture.
- **The only protection:** Line 5's shared idempotency key is the only thing that could make those retries safe. That works only if the endpoint treats repeated requests with the same key as one capture.
- **That protection is unconfirmed:** Line 6 says this behaviour "has not been checked, and the payments team has not confirmed it."

So the plan's own text shows that its central safety rule depends on an unchecked property. Retries are enabled anyway. The plan cannot claim line 2 holds until the payments team confirms deduplication on that endpoint.

**What is and isn't proven:**

- The finding is labelled VERIFIED because the snapshot itself states the gap (line 6).
- It is not proven that the endpoint actually double-charges. That would need evidence outside the snapshot.
- This is not just a missing piece of supporting evidence. The plan's design conflicts with its own rule unless the unchecked behaviour turns out to be true.

Line 7 removes library-level retries, which is good but does not address this gap. The backoff schedule in line 3 does not affect the blocker either.
