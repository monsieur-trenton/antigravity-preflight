**Rationale**

The arithmetic is internally consistent:
- 240,000 requests at 5 requests per second is 48,000 seconds, about 13.3 hours.
- The peak is 5 + 3 = 8 requests per second.
- The daily maximum is 240,000 + 50,000 = 290,000 requests.
- Not retrying failed lookups keeps the job at or below 240,000 requests.

The blocker is a conflict between two of the plan's own lines. Line 2 makes a hard requirement: the account must stay within its rate limits at all times, because a breach suspends the account and also cuts off live signup lookups. Line 6 says nobody has confirmed which limits apply and that the plan has no figure for them. The plan therefore cannot show that 8 requests per second or 290,000 requests per day meets its own requirement.

This is not a claim that the limits will be exceeded. The defect is that the plan's central safety condition cannot be checked against anything, and a failure would break signup. The plan needs the provider's actual per-second and per-day limits, with headroom, before the run goes ahead.
