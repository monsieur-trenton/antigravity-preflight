**Rationale**

The only way two locks can deadlock is if two holders acquire them in opposite orders, or if a holder never releases one. Neither happens in the snapshot.

- **Consistent lock order.** Checkout (line 2) and restock (line 3) both acquire stock_mu first and reserve_mu second, then release both. They often run at the same time (line 4), but with the same order neither can hold reserve_mu while waiting for stock_mu. So they cannot form a cycle.
- **The audit job cannot cause a deadlock.** It holds stock_mu alone for at most 2 seconds and never requests another lock (line 5). It cannot take part in a cycle, and its hold time is bounded.
- **No other lock users.** Line 6 rules out any other acquisition site that could reverse the order.
- **The requirement is met.** Line 7 forbids any deadlock, and the design above meets that from the snapshot's own text.

Blocking with no timeout (line 1) can make checkout and restock wait up to about 2 seconds during the nightly audit. That is a delay, not a deadlock, and the snapshot sets no latency requirement, so it is not a finding. No blocker, risk or needed change is supported by the snapshot.
