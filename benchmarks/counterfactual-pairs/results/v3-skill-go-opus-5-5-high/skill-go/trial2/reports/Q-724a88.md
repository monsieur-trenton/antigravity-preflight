**Rationale**

The plan contradicts its own rate requirement. Line 3 runs 12 independent workers at a fixed count with no shared coordination. Line 4 caps each worker at 5 requests in any one-second window. Together that allows up to 12 × 5 = 60 requests per second.

Line 2 sets the contract limit at 50 requests per second in total. One second over that limit suspends the key for 24 hours. Line 7 requires the backfill to stay within the contracted rate, because a suspension would also stop live checkout lookups.

The other lines don't close the gap:
- Retries share the per-worker limiter (line 5), so they don't add traffic beyond 60, but they don't lower the ceiling either.
- No other service uses the key (line 6), so 60 requests per second is the actual worst case, not an underestimate.
- With 3.2 million rows, all 12 workers will be busy at the same time for most of the run. Hitting the ceiling is the expected steady state, not a rare edge case.

The arithmetic is entirely in the snapshot, so this is a verified blocker. A fix would be to keep the per-worker cap at 4 or below (12 × 4 = 48), reduce the worker count, or use a shared limiter across workers.
