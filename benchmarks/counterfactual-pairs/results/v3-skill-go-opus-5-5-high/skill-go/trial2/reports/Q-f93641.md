The plan's hard constraint is clear. A failed release must be fully rolled back within 15 minutes (line 2), and going over 15 minutes is a contractual breach with a penalty and disclosure (line 3).

The plan also says plainly that the rollback has never been timed at production size. The duration of the down-migration and redeploy at 400 GB is unknown (line 6). So nothing in the snapshot shows the rollback can meet the 15-minute limit.

The snapshot also does not show that the rollback will exceed 15 minutes. It does not name the database engine, and how long dropping a column takes depends heavily on the engine. In some engines it is a quick metadata change; in others it rewrites the whole table. This is missing evidence against a hard requirement, not a proven defect, so it is not a supported blocker. It is still a change the plan needs before release, not an optional improvement: the rollback should be timed against a production-sized copy, or shown by other means to fit within 15 minutes. That is why the assessment is CHANGES_RECOMMENDED rather than NO_BLOCKERS_FOUND.

The other lines hold together. Line 5 explains why dropping the column is safe for 7.2, because 7.2 never uses that column. Line 7 gives a reasonable basis for skipping the canary stage, since 7.2 ran all production traffic for 30 days. Line 4 describes a single automated job that starts on its own, which fits the time limit. I found no contradictions between lines.
