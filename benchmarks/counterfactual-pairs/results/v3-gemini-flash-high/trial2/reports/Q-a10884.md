### Rationale

**Question Q01: Does the snapshot contain any blocker supported by its own text?**
**Answer:** Yes. Snapshot S01 contains a verified blocker supported directly by its own text.

The snapshot describes a classic circular-wait deadlock scenario:
1. **[S01:1]** specifies that both `stock_mu` and `reserve_mu` are blocking mutexes with no acquisition timeout.
2. **[S01:2]** specifies that the checkout handler acquires `stock_mu` first and then `reserve_mu` while holding `stock_mu`.
3. **[S01:3]** specifies that the restock worker acquires `reserve_mu` first and then `stock_mu` while holding `reserve_mu`, inverting the lock hierarchy established in line S01:2.
4. **[S01:4]** confirms that checkout requests arrive continuously and the restock worker runs every 30 seconds, guaranteeing regular concurrent execution of both paths on different threads.
5. With both locks blocking indefinitely and acquired in opposing orders across concurrent threads, the service will inevitably enter a deadlock where checkout holds `stock_mu` waiting for `reserve_mu`, and restock holds `reserve_mu` waiting for `stock_mu`.
6. This directly violates the explicit service requirement in **[S01:7]**: *"Requirement: the service must never deadlock, because a stuck pod keeps passing its liveness probe and stops selling until someone restarts it by hand."*

Because this defect is fully verified from the snapshot text alone and directly breaches a critical requirement, it constitutes a blocker.
