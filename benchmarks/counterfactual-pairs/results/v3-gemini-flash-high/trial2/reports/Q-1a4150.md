### Rationale and Analysis

A line-by-line inspection of Snapshot S01 reveals no blockers, internal conflicts, or invalid assumptions:

1. **Rate Limit Bounds and Concurrency**:
   - The vendor geocoding API contract permits at most 50 requests per second in total, with exceeding this in any one-second window resulting in a 24-hour key suspension ([S01:2]).
   - The workload runs a fixed pool of 8 worker processes in parallel ([S01:3]).
   - Each worker process is governed by a client-side limiter capping throughput to at most 5 requests in any one-second window ([S01:4]).
   - Consequently, the maximum possible aggregate request rate across all workers in any one-second window is $8 \times 5 = 40$ requests per second. Because $40 \le 50$, the maximum throughput remains strictly within the contract limit.

2. **Absence of Shared Coordination**:
   - The lack of shared coordination between workers ([S01:3]) does not introduce a race condition or risk of exceeding the limit, because the static partitioning ($8 \times 5 = 40\text{ req/s}$) guarantees that even simultaneous peak bursts across all workers cannot exceed 50 requests in any one-second window.
   - The worker process count remains fixed throughout the backfill run ([S01:3]).

3. **Retry Overhead**:
   - Retries for HTTP 500 responses consume capacity from the same client-side limiter as initial requests ([S01:5]). Thus, retries cannot generate burst traffic beyond the 5 requests/sec ceiling per worker.

4. **API Key Isolation and Impact**:
   - No other job or service shares or uses the API key during the backfill execution ([S01:6]).
   - The business requirement that the backfill remain within the contracted rate to avoid stopping live checkout address lookups ([S01:7]) is satisfied by the design.

Because the plan parameters mathematically prevent exceeding the contracted rate limit and no other traffic shares the key, no blockers, defects, or conflicts are present.
