# Review: Nightly Batch Plan

**The plan fits the 75-minute deadline with room to spare, even in the worst observed case.**

## Timing

| Stage | Typical | Worst observed (30 runs) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Import | 22 min | 25 min |
| 2. Transform | 18 min | 21 min |
| 3. Export | 12 min | 14 min |
| **Total (sequential)** | **52 min** | **60 min** |

- **Typical run:** 52 min, leaving 23 min (about 31%) of headroom.
- **Worst case:** 60 min, leaving 15 min (20%) of headroom. This adds up each stage's worst time as if all three happened in one run. That probably never happened in the 30 runs, so 60 minutes is a pessimistic figure.

Because the stages run one after another and never overlap, adding their times is the right way to get the total. Nothing in the plan breaks the deadline rule.

## Summary email

The email is sent after the batch ends. The plan doesn't say whether this step counts toward the 75 minutes. Sending one email takes seconds, so it doesn't change the result either way.

## Optional suggestions (not required)

These would make the plan more robust, but it works without them:

1. **Watch for slow growth.** Thirty runs is a modest sample. If data volume grows, the 15-minute buffer could shrink without anyone noticing. A trend alert, for example at 65 minutes total, would catch this early.
2. **Lose less on a kill.** Today, an overrun loses the whole day's data. Saving each stage's output as it finishes would allow a partial rerun instead of losing everything.
3. **Warn early.** Alert on-call if any stage runs past its observed maximum. That gives people time to step in before the deadline hits.
4. **Report failures too.** Make sure a summary or alert also goes out if the batch is killed or fails, not only when it finishes normally.

## Conclusion

The plan works as written and keeps a comfortable margin under the hard deadline. Everything above is optional hardening, not a needed change.
