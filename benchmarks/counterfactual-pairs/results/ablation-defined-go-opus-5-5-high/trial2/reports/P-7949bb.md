# Plan Review: Service Call Timeout and Retry

## Unit check

- Client library X takes its timeout in **milliseconds**.
- `timeout=5000` therefore means **5000 ms = 5 s**, which matches the stated intent of 5 seconds.
- 5 s is 2.5× the documented upstream maximum of 2 s and over 6× the p99 of 800 ms. Healthy calls won't time out spuriously.

The units are consistent, and the value does what the plan says it does.

## Retry logic

- The call is **idempotent**, so retrying on timeout is safe. A request that actually completed upstream but timed out on our side won't cause duplicate side effects.
- Up to 3 retries with exponential backoff is a conventional, bounded policy. It won't hammer the upstream in a tight loop.

## Concurrency

- 16 worker threads is a modest pool, and nothing in the plan conflicts with it.
- The worst case for one logical call is 4 attempts × 5 s plus backoff, roughly 20+ s holding one thread.
- That only happens if the upstream is badly degraded, beyond its documented maximum. The plan still behaves correctly then: it is bounded and eventually fails.

## Optional robustness suggestions (not required)

- **Add jitter** to the exponential backoff. This prevents 16 threads that time out together from retrying in lockstep.
- **Set an overall deadline** per logical operation, e.g. 10–12 s across all attempts. This caps how long a thread can be tied up during an upstream outage.
- **Use a circuit breaker or retry budget.** In a sustained outage it would fail fast instead of tying up all 16 workers in retry cycles.

These only add resilience. The plan is correct as written.
