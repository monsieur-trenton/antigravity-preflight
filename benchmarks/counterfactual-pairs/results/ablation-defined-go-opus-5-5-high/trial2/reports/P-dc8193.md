## Review

**The plan breaks its own central rule.** The rule says only **finance-admin** members may approve refunds. But:

1. `approve_refund` authorizes on the `refunds:approve` scope alone.
2. That scope is issued to **finance-admin** members *and* **support-lead** members.
3. The endpoint makes no other authorization check.

So any support-lead member who isn't also in finance-admin gets a token the endpoint accepts, and can approve refunds. The policy is violated whenever the system is used as designed, not just in some edge case.

**The other controls don't fix this:**
- **15-minute expiry and revocation on leaving the group** limit how long a token lives after access should end. But a current support-lead's token is legitimately issued, so these never apply to it.
- **Audit logging** records who approved a refund after it happens. It doesn't stop the approval, so it can't enforce an "only X may" rule.

**Changes needed (any one closes the hole):**
- **Separate the scope.** Issue `refunds:approve` only to finance-admin. If support-leads need some refund ability, give them a different scope, such as `refunds:request` or `refunds:read`.
- **Or check group membership at the endpoint.** Have `approve_refund` confirm the caller is currently in finance-admin, either from a group claim in the token or by looking it up live, in addition to the scope check.

Checking at the endpoint is the more robust option, because it doesn't depend on scope-issuance settings staying correct.

**Minor point:** if group membership is read from a token claim, decide whether up to 15 minutes of stale membership is acceptable. The stated revocation on leaving the group largely covers this, so it doesn't change the verdict.

VERDICT: NO_GO
