# Plan Review: Service Call Timeout Configuration

## Critical issue: unit mismatch in the timeout

Client library X takes its timeout in **milliseconds**, so `timeout=5` means **5 ms**, not the intended 5 seconds. The plan's stated goal is a timeout "comfortably above the upstream's documented maximum" of 2 seconds. This setting is 400× too short to meet it.

**What happens in practice:**
- The upstream's p99 is 800 ms, and its typical latency is almost certainly well above 5 ms once you count network round-trip. Nearly every call will time out.
- Each timeout triggers a retry, and every retry also times out after 5 ms. Each logical request becomes 4 failed attempts plus backoff delays.
- The 16 worker threads spend their time on doomed attempts and backoff sleeps, which cuts throughput.
- The upstream may still be processing the abandoned requests. Up to 4× the load can land on it, and the client gets no successful responses back.

Because the call is idempotent, the retries won't corrupt data. But the call will fail almost always, which defeats the plan's purpose.

**Required fix:** set `timeout=5000`. Better still, use a named constant or a duration type, such as `TIMEOUT_MS = 5000` or `timeout=Duration.ofSeconds(5)` if the library supports one. That makes the unit explicit and prevents this class of error from recurring.

## Secondary observations (after the fix)

- **Worst-case latency budget:** with a 5 s timeout and 3 retries, one logical call can take about 20 s plus backoff. Check that this fits within any caller or end-to-end deadline. If the caller has its own deadline, consider capping total retry time.
- **Timeout headroom:** 5 s against a documented 2 s maximum is reasonable. A tighter value such as 2.5–3 s would free threads sooner when the upstream misbehaves, but this is a tuning choice.
- **Backoff jitter:** add jitter to the exponential backoff so the 16 threads don't retry in lockstep after a shared upstream hiccup.

These are robustness suggestions. The required change is the unit fix.

## Summary

As written, the timeout is 5 ms rather than 5 s. Nearly every call will time out, retry, and fail, so the plan does not achieve its own goal.
