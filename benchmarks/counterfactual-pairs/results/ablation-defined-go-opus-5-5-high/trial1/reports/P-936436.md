# Review: build step stdout and the 1500-byte cap

**Does the summary fit under the cap?** Yes, with a large margin. Here is the worst case for compact JSON with 6 keys:

| Component | Worst case |
|---|---|
| Key: ≤12 bytes plus 2 quotes | 14 B × 6 = 84 B |
| Colon separator | 1 B × 6 = 6 B |
| Value: ≤64 bytes escaped plus 2 quotes (anything longer becomes `"too long"`, 10 B) | 66 B × 6 = 396 B |
| Commas (5) and braces (2) | 7 B |
| Optional trailing newline | 1 B |
| **Total** | **≤ 494 B** |

That is under the stated 600-byte bound and about a third of the 1500-byte cap.

- Because the values are ASCII-escaped, character count equals byte count. Multi-byte UTF-8 cannot inflate the size.
- The 64-byte limit is applied to the escaped form, so escape expansion (`\"`, `\uXXXX`) is already counted.
- The keys are fixed and at most 12 bytes, so they are bounded.

**Is the failure mode handled?** Yes. An overflow aborts the release with no retry, so a hard check matters. The plan has two layers:
1. The size bound above.
2. An assertion on the encoded byte length before anything is written. It checks bytes, not characters, which is the correct measure. It also runs before writing, so an oversized summary never reaches stdout.

**Debug log:** It goes to a temp file, never to stdout, and is removed on exit. It cannot affect the cap.

**Optional suggestions (not required):**
- Make sure nothing else in the step prints to stdout, such as tool banners or warnings from subprocesses. The assertion only covers the summary itself. Redirecting child process stdout to the debug log would rule this out.
- You could tighten the assertion to the real 600-byte design bound. A failure would then point to a logic bug rather than a near-miss on the cap.

The plan works as written and stays within its own rules.
