# Review: Nightly Compaction Plan

**Timing check.** 40 GB at 55 MB/s is about 727 s (decimal units) or 745 s (binary units). Either way that's roughly 12 minutes, so the plan's figure is correct. Against a 30-minute window, that leaves about 2.5× headroom. The rate was measured on production-class hardware, so the estimate rests on real data.

**Failure handling.** If the window runs out, the step is cancelled and the cancellation is reported. An overrun is therefore a visible, bounded event, not a silent hang or a step that bleeds into whatever runs next.

**Single thread.** This is consistent with the measurement. The 55 MB/s figure presumably came from the same single-threaded setup, so nothing in the estimate assumes parallelism the plan doesn't use.

**Per-minute logging.** A ~12-minute run produces about 12 progress lines, and a run that hits the timeout produces about 30. That's enough to tell slow progress from a stall, at negligible cost.

**Internal consistency.** No stated rule is violated, and the numbers support the conclusion. Nothing in the plan as written will fail.

**Optional suggestions** (these improve robustness but aren't required):
- Track input size over time. At 55 MB/s, the 30-minute window holds about 99 GB, so input could grow about 2.5× before overruns become routine.
- Re-measure throughput under typical nightly contention, such as backups or other batch jobs, in case the night-time rate is lower than the benchmark.
- Alert on repeated cancellations, not just individual ones.

None of these are needed for the plan to work.
