The plan is sound as written. Here are the checks I made:

**Timeout units and value**
- Library X takes milliseconds, so `timeout=5000` is 5 seconds, which matches the stated intent.
- 5 s is 2.5× the documented 2 s maximum and over 6× the p99 of 800 ms. Legitimate responses won't be cut off, and timeouts will only fire when the upstream is actually misbehaving.

**Retry policy**
- Retrying on timeout is safe because the call is idempotent. Duplicate execution after a timeout whose request actually reached the server causes no harm.
- Three retries with exponential backoff is a conventional, bounded policy.

**Worker pool**
- With 16 threads, the worst case for one task is about 4 attempts × 5 s plus backoff, so a bit over 20 s. That is bounded, and nothing in the plan says it violates a latency or throughput requirement. The pool degrades during an upstream outage but doesn't deadlock or grow without limit.

**Optional hardening (not required)**
- **Jitter** on the backoff would keep 16 threads that time out together from retrying in lockstep.
- **An overall deadline** per logical call would cap the total time spent, rather than relying only on per-attempt timeouts.
- **A circuit breaker** or retry budget would stop the pool from piling retries onto an upstream that is already failing. Since timeouts only happen when the upstream is beyond its documented maximum, they are a strong sign of trouble.
- **Separate connect and read timeouts** would help if library X supports them, so an unreachable host fails fast instead of waiting the full 5 s.

None of these fixes a defect. They only make a working plan more resilient.
