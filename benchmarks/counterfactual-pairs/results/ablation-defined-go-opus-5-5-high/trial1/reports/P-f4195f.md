## Review

**Timing check against the 75-minute hard deadline**

| Stage | Typical | Observed max (last 30 runs) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Import | 22 min | 25 min |
| 2. Transform | 18 min | 21 min |
| 3. Export | 12 min | 14 min |
| **Total (sequential)** | **52 min** | **60 min** |

- **Typical runs:** 52 minutes, leaving 23 minutes (about 31%) of headroom.
- **Worst case:** If every stage hits its observed maximum in the same run, the total is 60 minutes. That still leaves 15 minutes (20%) of margin. This is a pessimistic bound, since all three maxima are unlikely to land on the same night.
- The stages run in sequence and don't overlap, so adding them up is the right way to compute the total.
- The summary email is sent after the batch ends. It's a small step and doesn't threaten the deadline.

The plan meets its one stated hard rule, even under the worst observed conditions. Nothing as written will fail or break a constraint.

**Optional robustness suggestions** (not required changes)

1. **Add a margin alert.** For example, warn on-call if the cumulative runtime passes about 65 minutes. That gives a chance to act before the kill at 75. Thirty runs is a modest sample, and data volumes tend to grow over time.
2. **Track trends.** Graph per-stage durations so slow growth (e.g. import creeping up as data grows) shows up well before the margin is gone.
3. **Report failures in the email.** If the run is killed, the "batch ends" email may never be sent. A separate watchdog alert for killed or overrun runs would make sure the data loss gets noticed.
4. **Consider checkpointing or rerun ability.** If stage outputs were saved, a killed run could resume instead of losing the whole day's data.

None of these are needed for the plan to work. They only add resilience.
