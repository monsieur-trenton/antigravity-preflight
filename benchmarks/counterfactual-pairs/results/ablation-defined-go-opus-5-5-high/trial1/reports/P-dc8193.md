## Review

**The rule:** Only members of `finance-admin` may approve refunds.

**What the plan actually enforces:**
1. `approve_refund` admits any caller whose token has the `refunds:approve` scope.
2. That scope is issued to members of **both** `finance-admin` **and** `support-lead`.
3. The endpoint does no other authorization check.

Together, these mean a `support-lead` member who is not in `finance-admin` gets a token with `refunds:approve`, calls the endpoint, and is let in. That is a direct violation of the rule the plan states. This isn't an edge case or a hardening gap. It is the normal, designed path for every support-lead.

**Why the other controls don't fix it:**
- **15-minute expiry and revocation on group exit:** These limit how long a token stays valid. But a support-lead's scope is legitimately "justified" by their support-lead membership, so it is never revoked while they remain support-lead. The window is short but renews indefinitely.
- **Audit logging:** This records the unauthorized approval after it happens. It is detective, not preventive, so the rule is still broken.

**Required fix (one of):**
- Stop issuing `refunds:approve` to `support-lead`. If support-leads need some refund capability, give them a separate scope (e.g. `refunds:request` or `refunds:read`) that the approve endpoint does not accept.
- Or add a check in `approve_refund` that the caller is currently a member of `finance-admin`, rather than relying on the scope alone. Ideally it would check against the authoritative group source rather than a claim baked into the token.

The first option is cleaner because it keeps the scope's meaning aligned with the policy. The second is useful as defense in depth anyway.

**Minor note:** It would help to specify whether the audit log records the group membership that justified the approval, not just the identity. That makes violations like this one detectable after the fact. This is optional, though.

VERDICT: NO_GO
