## Risks and gaps

**1. Retries have no overall time limit (RISK, line 4).**
- "Up to 3 times" plausibly means 4 attempts, but the wording could also count the first call.
- Assuming 4 attempts, a single call that keeps timing out could hold a thread for about 20 seconds plus backoff delays.
- The plan sets no overall deadline and gives no backoff base, cap or jitter.
- This is a design risk, not a proven defect. Whether 20+ seconds is acceptable depends on caller requirements the snapshot doesn't state.

**2. The thread pool could fill up when the upstream is slow (RISK, line 5).**
- With only 16 workers, a degraded upstream could tie up every thread in long timeout-and-retry cycles.
- Those threads would then send repeated retries to a service that is already struggling.
- The plan mentions no circuit breaker or concurrency limit.
- This is inferred from lines 4 and 5 together, not demonstrated.

**3. The millisecond unit can't be confirmed here (NOTE, line 1).**
- Line 3 is only correct if line 1 is right about library X using milliseconds.
- The snapshot asserts this without evidence such as library documentation.
- It is also unclear whether the timeout covers connecting, reading, or the whole request.
