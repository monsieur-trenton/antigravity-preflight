# Review: Nightly Compaction Plan

## Summary

The plan is internally consistent. It sets a hard limit (30 minutes), says what happens when the limit is hit (cancel and report), and logs progress. Nothing in it breaks a rule it states. The main weakness is that **nobody knows whether the job can finish in the window.** The plan handles overrun safely, but it may overrun every night.

## The core math

Processing 40 GB in 30 minutes needs a sustained rate of about **22–23 MB/s** (40 GB ÷ 1800 s). On a single thread, that may or may not be achievable. It depends on:

- disk read and write speed on the target host,
- how much CPU the compaction algorithm needs per byte,
- any contention from other nightly jobs.

The plan admits throughput "has not been measured." So the margin is unknown. It might be generous, or it might be negative.

## What works

- **Bounded runtime with defined failure behavior.** Cancellation is reported, not silent, so overruns will be visible.
- **Per-minute progress logs** give enough detail to diagnose a slow run afterward.
- **Single thread** keeps the step simple and predictable on a shared host.

## Recommended changes

1. **Measure throughput before relying on the window.** Run a benchmark on the target host, or a test run on a representative sample (e.g., 2–4 GB). Record MB/s and extrapolate. This is the most important missing piece.
2. **Log rate and estimated finish time, not just progress.** If each per-minute line includes MB/s and projected completion, an overrun becomes predictable a few minutes in, not only at minute 30.
3. **Define what cancellation leaves behind.** The plan says the step is cancelled but not what state the data is in. Specify one of these:
   - the original input stays authoritative and partial output is discarded or rolled back, or
   - compaction checkpoints its progress and resumes the next night.

   Without this, a cancelled run could leave half-compacted or inconsistent data.
4. **Track repeated cancellations.** One overrun is fine. Several nights in a row means compaction never completes and the backlog grows. Alert after N consecutive cancellations, or when the input grows past a threshold.
5. **Plan for input growth.** "Roughly 40 GB" today may be 60 GB in a few months. Note how much headroom the measured throughput gives. Also note the fallback if it runs out: parallel workers, a longer window, or incremental compaction.
6. **Optional: check for early abort.** If the rate at minute 5 shows the job cannot finish, decide whether to stop early and free the host, or keep going for the partial progress (only useful if it is resumable).

## Why not NO_GO

The plan does not guarantee failure. A single thread can plausibly reach about 23 MB/s, and the overrun case is explicitly handled. Why not GO: the missing throughput measurement and the undefined cancellation state are real gaps that should be closed before the plan is trusted in production.

VERDICT: GO_WITH_CHANGES
