# Review: Nightly Batch Plan

## Timing check

| Stage | Typical | Observed max (30 runs) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Import | 22 min | 25 min |
| 2. Transform | 18 min | 21 min |
| 3. Export | 12 min | 14 min |
| **Total (sequential)** | **52 min** | **60 min** |

- **Typical run:** 52 min, leaving 23 min of headroom under the 75-minute limit.
- **Worst case (every stage at its max at once):** 60 min, leaving 15 min of headroom.

The worst-case total assumes all three stages hit their maximum in the same run, which is unlikely. Even so, the batch still finishes 15 minutes early. As written, the plan meets its stated deadline, so it is not a NO_GO.

## Issues worth fixing

1. **Losing a whole day's data on overrun is a big risk with no mitigation.** The plan has no checkpointing, no retry, and no recovery path. A rare overrun (for example, a slow upstream source or a 2× spike in data volume) would lose the whole day. Consider:
   - persisting each stage's output so a killed run can resume from the last completed stage;
   - a fallback that keeps the import output, even if transform and export have to rerun the next day.

2. **The summary email only fires "when the batch ends."** If the job is killed at 75 minutes or crashes mid-stage, it is unclear whether on-call hears anything. The failure case most needs the alert. Send the notification from a supervisor or wrapper that runs whatever the outcome, and include success or failure plus each stage's duration.

3. **There is no early-warning threshold.** Thirty runs is a fairly small sample, and data volumes usually grow. Add alerts on cumulative elapsed time, such as:
   - a warning if stages 1 and 2 together exceed about 50 minutes;
   - a warning if the total exceeds 65 minutes.

   This catches drift before it hits the limit. Also track per-stage durations over time to spot trends.

4. **The scheduling assumptions are unstated.** The plan does not say:
   - when the batch starts;
   - whether the 75-minute window includes queueing, startup, or email time;
   - what happens if the previous night's run or another job is still holding resources.

   Each of these could eat into the 15-minute margin.

5. **There are no error-handling rules.** The plan does not say whether a failed stage retries, and if so, whether the retry fits within the remaining time. For example, a single retry of import (up to 25 min) on top of a worst-case run would reach 85 minutes and overrun the deadline.

## Summary

The plan is feasible: even a worst-case run at 60 minutes fits comfortably within the 75-minute limit. However, the cost of failure is high and there is little observability. Better alerting, checkpointing, and explicit retry and timing rules would make it much more robust.
