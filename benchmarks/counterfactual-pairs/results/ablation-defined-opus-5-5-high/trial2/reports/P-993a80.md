# Review: Refund Approval Authorization Plan

## Does the plan meet its stated rule?

The rule is that **only finance-admin members may approve refunds.** The plan enforces it through this chain:

1. The endpoint admits only tokens with `refunds:approve`.
2. That scope is issued only to finance-admin members.
3. A token is revoked as soon as its holder leaves finance-admin, and expires after 15 minutes anyway.

If each statement holds, every accepted caller is a current finance-admin member. The plan is logically sound, so it is not a NO_GO. But the whole rule rests on a single check at the endpoint and on assumptions about other systems, and the plan should be strengthened.

## Concerns and recommended changes

**1. The endpoint must actually honor revocation.**
"Revoked as soon as its holder leaves" only works if the endpoint rejects revoked tokens. If tokens are self-contained (for example, JWTs checked by signature alone), revocation does nothing at the endpoint. A removed user could keep approving refunds for up to 15 minutes.
- *Change:* State how the endpoint checks revocation, such as token introspection or a revocation list. Otherwise, explicitly accept the 15-minute window as a known risk.

**2. The issuance invariant lives entirely outside the endpoint.**
The scope reliably means finance-admin membership only if every token-issuing path enforces that. This includes token exchange, service accounts, admin or impersonation tooling, test or staging issuers, and future scope-mapping changes. One misconfigured client or role mapping silently breaks the rule, and nothing downstream would catch it.
- *Change:* Add defense in depth. The endpoint should also verify finance-admin membership, either from a group claim or a directory lookup. At minimum, add tests and monitoring that alert when `refunds:approve` appears on a token whose subject is not in finance-admin.

**3. Separation of duties is not addressed.**
Nothing stops a finance-admin member from approving a refund they requested or that benefits them. This is beyond the stated rule, but it is a common control for refunds.
- *Change:* Consider blocking self-approval, or requiring a second approver above a threshold.

**4. Audit log integrity.**
Recording identity is good. Make the log append-only or tamper-evident, and include the token ID, the scope, and the group membership at the time of approval. That way you can confirm after the fact that the rule held for each approval.

**5. Token-type ambiguity.**
Specify that only user-bound tokens qualify. If machine-to-machine tokens can carry this scope, there may be no "member" behind the call at all.

## Summary

The plan works as written if its premises hold, so it does not fail or break its rule. However, it relies on a single scope check whose meaning is guaranteed elsewhere. Adding a direct membership check, confirming the endpoint enforces revocation, and hardening the audit trail would make the rule robust.

VERDICT: GO_WITH_CHANGES
