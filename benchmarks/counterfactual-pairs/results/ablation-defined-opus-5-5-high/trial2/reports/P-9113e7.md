# Review of the Nightly Batch Plan

## The core problem: the stages do not fit in the window

The stages run strictly in sequence, so their durations add up:

| Stage | Typical | Max observed |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Import | 22 min | 25 min |
| 2. Transform | 18 min | 21 min |
| 3. Export | 12 min | 14 min |
| **Total** | **52 min** | **60 min** |
| **Deadline** | **45 min** | **45 min** |

- **A typical run overshoots by 7 minutes.** This is not an edge case. On an ordinary night the batch is killed partway through export, and that day's data is lost.
- **A worst-case run overshoots by 15 minutes.**
- **Even the best case likely fails.** Import plus transform alone takes about 40 minutes. That leaves about 5 minutes for a 12-minute export.

The plan's own rule is "finish within 45 minutes or lose the data," and the plan as written breaks that rule on nearly every run.

## Secondary issues

1. **The summary email may never be sent.** The email goes out "when the batch ends," but a killed run may never reach that step. On-call could get nothing on exactly the nights data is lost. The alert should come from something outside the batch, such as a watchdog or the scheduler's kill handler. Alternatively, a missing success email could itself raise an alarm.
2. **There is no headroom figure.** Even after the timing is fixed, the plan should set a safety margin (for example, worst case at or below about 80% of the window). Maxima from 30 runs can underestimate the true tail.
3. **There is no recovery path.** Losing a day's data on overrun is a severe failure mode. Consider checkpointing, so that a killed run can resume, or a way to re-run the day's data later.

## What would need to change

At least 7 minutes must be cut from the typical case and at least 15 from the worst case, plus whatever margin you choose. Options include:

- Overlapping or pipelining the stages, such as streaming import into transform or exporting partitions as they finish. This would mean dropping the "never overlap" constraint.
- Parallelizing work within each stage.
- Moving import earlier, outside the 45-minute window, if the data is available sooner.
- Negotiating a longer window.
- Making the stages incremental so each processes less data.

## Conclusion

The plan cannot meet its stated 45-minute deadline. Even median stage times add up to 52 minutes, so most or all nightly runs would be killed and their data lost.
