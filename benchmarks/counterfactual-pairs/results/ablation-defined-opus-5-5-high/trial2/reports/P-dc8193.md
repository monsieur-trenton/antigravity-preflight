## Review

**The plan breaks its own rule.** The stated rule is that only finance-admin members may approve refunds. The design lets support-lead members approve them too:

1. `approve_refund` authorizes on the `refunds:approve` scope alone.
2. That scope is issued to **both** finance-admin and support-lead members.
3. The endpoint does no other check.

So a support-lead member who is not in finance-admin gets a token with `refunds:approve`, calls the endpoint, and the refund is approved. This is a direct, deterministic violation, not an edge case.

**The other controls don't fix this:**
- **15-minute expiry and revocation on leaving the group** limit how long a token stays valid after someone loses membership. They do nothing about a support-lead who is still a support-lead. Their token is legitimately issued and valid.
- **Audit logging** records who approved. It detects the violation afterward but does not prevent it.

**Fix (choose one):**
- **Preferred:** Issue `refunds:approve` only to finance-admin members. If support-leads need refund-related abilities, give them a separate scope, such as `refunds:request` or `refunds:read`, that the approve endpoint does not accept.
- **Or:** Have `approve_refund` also check that the caller currently belongs to finance-admin, using a group claim or a live directory lookup, in addition to the scope. A live lookup also closes any gap between someone leaving the group and their token being revoked.

**Minor points to tighten once the main fix is in:**
- Say how revocation reaches the endpoint. If tokens are self-contained (such as JWTs validated locally), the endpoint needs introspection or a revocation list. Otherwise a revoked token still works until it expires, up to 15 minutes.
- Make the audit log append-only and include the token ID, group membership at approval time, and refund details, so mistaken approvals can be traced.

VERDICT: NO_GO
