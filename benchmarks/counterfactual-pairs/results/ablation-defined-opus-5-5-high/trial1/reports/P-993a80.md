# Review: Refund Approval Authorization Plan

## Does the plan satisfy its stated rule?

The rule is that only finance-admin members may approve refunds. Taking the plan's statements as given, the chain holds:

1. The endpoint accepts only tokens with `refunds:approve`.
2. That scope is issued only to finance-admin members.
3. A token is revoked as soon as its holder leaves finance-admin.

So every token that can reach the approval logic belongs to a current finance-admin member. The 15-minute expiry and audit log are extra protection on top of that. The plan works as written, so it is not NO_GO. Several assumptions are implicit, though, and should be made explicit and tested.

## Recommended changes

**1. Make the endpoint actually enforce revocation.**
The plan says tokens are "revoked," but the endpoint "does no other authorization check." If tokens are self-contained (for example, JWTs checked only by signature and expiry), revocation does nothing at this endpoint. A removed member could keep approving refunds for up to 15 minutes. State that the endpoint checks token status on every request, through introspection or a revocation list, or else use opaque tokens. Without this, step 3 above is only a claim.

**2. Close every other way to obtain the scope.**
Step 2 is the whole security model. Confirm the scope cannot be gained through:
- token exchange or delegation flows that carry scopes over,
- service or machine accounts,
- client-credentials grants,
- admin or impersonation tooling,
- refresh flows that re-issue scopes without re-checking group membership.

Add a test or policy check showing the issuer is the only source of the scope and that it checks membership every time it issues or refreshes a token.

**3. Consider a membership check at request time.**
Checking finance-admin membership directly at the endpoint, in addition to the scope, costs little. It removes the dependence on revocation timing and on the issuer being correct. A single check at the enforcement point is fragile for a financial operation.

**4. Make the audit log tamper-resistant and complete.**
Record the token ID, the refund ID and amount, a timestamp, and the result, including denied attempts. Keep the log append-only and out of reach of finance-admin members, so approvers cannot change the record of their own actions.

**5. Separation of duties (optional, outside the stated rule).**
Nothing stops a finance-admin member from approving a refund they requested or one that benefits them. Consider blocking self-approval and requiring two approvers above a threshold amount.

**6. Define what happens on failure.**
Specify that if the revocation or introspection service is unavailable, the endpoint rejects the request rather than allowing it.

## Summary

The design is sound and meets its own rule under its stated assumptions. The biggest weakness is that the plan says nothing about how revocation is enforced at the endpoint, and step 2 relies entirely on the token issuer being correct. Making both explicit and adding a request-time check would strengthen the plan a lot.

VERDICT: GO_WITH_CHANGES
