# Review: Nightly Compaction Plan

**The arithmetic is correct.** 40 GB at 55 MB/s is about 727 seconds (decimal units) or about 745 seconds (binary units). Either way that is roughly 12 minutes. Against a 30-minute window, that leaves about 2.5× headroom. The figure comes from a measurement on a production-class host, so it isn't just an estimate.

**The plan doesn't contradict itself.** A single thread fits the measured rate. Per-minute logging gives about 12 progress lines in a normal run, and at most 30 before the window closes. Cancellation and reporting cover the timeout case.

Nothing here will fail as written. These gaps are worth closing:

1. **Partial output on cancellation.** The plan says the step is cancelled and reported, but not what happens to half-compacted data. It should state whether compaction writes to a temporary location and swaps atomically on success, or whether the next run can safely resume or restart. Without this, a cancelled run could leave inconsistent state.

2. **Input growth.** The 2.5× headroom applies to 40 GB today. A single thread can't scale, so growth eats the margin directly; the window is reached at about 95–99 GB. Log the input size and elapsed time each night, and alert well before the limit (for example, when a run exceeds 20 minutes).

3. **Throughput variance.** One measurement of 55 MB/s may not reflect nights with I/O contention from backups or other batch jobs. Record the actual rate in the per-minute progress logs (bytes done, MB/s) so a slowdown is visible before it causes a cancellation.

4. **What a cancellation report triggers.** Specify who receives the report, and whether repeated cancellations escalate. Each skipped compaction makes the next night's input larger, which can compound.

None of these block the plan, but item 1 in particular should be decided before rollout.
