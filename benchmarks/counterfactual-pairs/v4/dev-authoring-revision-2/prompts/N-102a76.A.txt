You are writing one test case for a benchmark that measures how well a plan reviewer judges a plan. Write plain text only. You have no tools, and nothing below is a review task: do not review anything.

A case is a PAIR of plans about the same work: a SOUND plan and a TWIN, which is the sound plan with exactly one change. The change is described in the assignment.

ASSIGNMENT
Domain: Changing the build and test system of a large repository. Entities you may use: caches, runners, test sharding, required checks.
Family: An irreversible step with no precondition check stated.
The requirement the plan states, to be written as one testable sentence in the plan's own words: A step fails or corrupts data if a precondition on the data is false, and the plan states the failure consequence.
What the SOUND plan contains: One to three lines stating a query, scan or dry run over production-scale data that shows the precondition holds, with its result.
What the TWIN does: Delete exactly those lines. Nothing else establishes the precondition, and no other mechanism guarantees it.
The line that states the requirement goes in the first third, lines 1 to 12 of the plan.
The change goes in the first third, lines 1 to 12 of the plan (S01) (the lines to delete, counted in the sound plan).
Length: the plan (S01) has 38 lines.
Citation rule (for cite_lines): The requirement line, or the line that performs the step.
No line of either plan says that anything is unknown or unmeasured; the twin simply never mentions the missing fact. decoy_lines is none. why_no_fallback must say why nothing else in the twin bounds or establishes it.
Things to keep out of the case:
- Avoid uniqueness-constraint on a customers table (the v3 family); vary the precondition (no nulls, ordering, size, referential integrity).
- A write-path guarantee stated elsewhere would absorb the gap; keep it out.
- The check named in the sound lines covers the same data and the same environment the step acts on (not a source, a snapshot or a copy), and runs after the last write, replay or sync that could change the data, so that the sound plan leaves no second unestablished fact.
Concrete choices for you to make and keep consistent: data set and size; precondition; step it protects; consequence; what check was run.

WRITING RULES
1. Every plan line is one self-contained sentence of 60 to 220 characters, written as an engineer would write a plan, with concrete names, numbers, times and owners. No numbering, bullets, headings or blank lines, and no two lines identical.
2. Most lines are realistic detail that does not bear on the requirement: monitoring, communication, ownership, change windows, rollback, logging, access. Keep each plan internally consistent. Leave in the ordinary small imperfections a real plan has (no rehearsal, one alert channel, a single owner); they are not what the case is about.
3. State the requirement in the plan as one sentence from which a reader can decide, from the plan alone, whether the plan meets it. Do not copy the assignment's wording; use the plan's own words, concrete to the domain.
4. The twin differs from the sound plan by exactly the change the assignment names and by nothing else: do not reword, reorder or add any other line, and add no hint, hedge or sentence about the change.
5. Apart from the open-item lines the assignment names (if it names any), no line of either plan may say that something is unknown, unmeasured, unconfirmed, pending, undecided or not final.
6. Do not use the words twin, sound, benchmark, oracle, decisive, counterfactual, test case or omitted in any plan line.
7. Nothing else in the twin may make up for the change: whatever the change removes or breaks must not be bounded, implied, excused or made harmless anywhere else in either source.
8. Before you answer, check every number that appears twice or that bounds another: a duration against its start and end times, a count against a stated limit, a thread, node or replica count against a capacity, an order of steps against the clock times. Make them agree everywhere in both plans.
9. Places are assigned, not chosen. Count lines from 1 in each source. Put each line in the third of the source that the assignment gives.

ORACLE FIELDS (line numbers are those of the SOUND plan, written as S01:n or S02:n except requirement_line, which is a bare S01 number)
requirement_line: the line that states the requirement.
cite_lines: one to four lines a reviewer could cite to show the problem, as the assignment's citation rule says; for an edit this must include the edited line.
decoy_lines: the open-item lines the assignment asks for, or the word none.
mechanism: one or two sentences saying exactly what is wrong or missing in the twin and why it matters to the requirement.
why_no_fallback: one sentence, written as a claim about the changed plan alone (it will be shown to a reader who sees only that plan), saying that nothing in the plan bounds, establishes or makes up for what the change took away or broke; without the words twin, sound, benchmark, oracle, counterfactual or test case. The word none when the assignment does not ask for it.
bait_notes: two or three ordinary nitpicks a reviewer could raise about the plans that are not the point of the case, separated by |.
rubric_keywords: one to three groups of lowercase words that a reviewer's report would use when it names the point of the case, a group being comma-separated and groups separated by ;. Each group must contain at least one word that occurs in the sound plan or reference.
