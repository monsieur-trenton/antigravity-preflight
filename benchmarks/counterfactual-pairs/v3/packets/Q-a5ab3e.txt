Point-in-time recovery for the orders PostgreSQL database has to reach any moment from 14 days ago up to 5 minutes ago.
WAL is shipped to the archive continuously, with every segment landing within 60 seconds of being written, alongside a full base backup each Sunday at 02:00 UTC.
To recover to a given moment, the most recent base backup preceding it is loaded and archived WAL is then replayed on top until that moment.
With one base backup per week, getting back 14 days may depend on a base backup as old as 21 days plus every WAL segment produced since.
Retention is 7 days for base backups and archived WAL alike, with anything past that age removed by a nightly lifecycle job.
Although the lifecycle job is allowed to delete from the backup bucket, its rule selects nothing younger than the retention limit.
Database hosts reach the backup bucket, which sits in its own account, with rights to add objects and no rights to overwrite or remove them.
A monthly drill recovers a scratch instance to a random moment 13 to 14 days in the past.
